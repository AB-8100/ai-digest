## 📐 ML & Technical Fundamentals

**Speculative Decoding — The Inference Trick That's Now Production-Standard**

Standard LLM inference has a fundamental bottleneck: the model generates one token per forward pass, making it inherently sequential and memory-bound. During inference, every new token requires a full forward pass through the model — this process is inherently sequential, meaning the model can't compute future tokens ahead even if the GPU is idle, leading to high inter-token latency and poor GPU utilisation.

Speculative decoding breaks this constraint. A small draft model proposes several tokens ahead, and then a large model verifies them all in a single forward pass. Correct predictions — typically 60–80% of tokens — get accepted at the inference cost of the small model. The outputs are mathematically identical to standard autoregressive decoding. Google uses speculative decoding in AI Overviews to serve over a billion Search users; Anthropic, Meta, and most major inference providers use it to reduce latency at scale, achieving 2–3x more tokens per second.

**Why it matters now for AI PMs**: If you're deploying LLMs in production in 2026, inference is almost certainly your biggest line item. A single unoptimised 70B model can cost $100+/hour on A100s; optimise properly and you're looking at $15–20/hour for equivalent throughput. Understanding speculative decoding lets you have an informed conversation with engineering about latency SLAs, cost projections, and model selection trade-offs.

**Recent development**: NVIDIA's [EAGLE-3 technique](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/an-introduction-to-speculative-decoding-for-reducing-latency-in-ai-inference/) advances speculative decoding further by training the draft model to use the target model's internal features — achieving higher acceptance rates than standard draft-model approaches. Speculative decoding breaks the bottleneck by generating multiple tokens in the time it normally takes to generate one, without changing the output distribution. Practical guides now exist for setup across llama.cpp, vLLM, SGLang, and TensorRT-LLM.

## ⚖️ Regulatory & Governance

**EU — AI Act Omnibus (Digital Omnibus on AI)** — The most material regulatory development of the summer. On 29 June, the Council of the EU gave its final green light to the AI Act simplification package, following the European Parliament's formal endorsement on 16 June 2026. The legislative act will be published in the EU's Official Journal shortly. Key changes:
- **High-risk AI (Annex III, use-based)**: obligations postponed from **2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027** — a 16-month deferral.
- **Transparency/watermarking (Article 50)**: for AI systems generating synthetic content placed on the EU market *before* 2 August 2026, the watermarking obligation is postponed from 2 August 2026 to **2 December 2026** — a 4-month grace period only.
- **SME simplification extended to mid-caps**: companies with up to 750 employees and €150M revenue now qualify for the simplified compliance framework, including reduced fines, regulatory sandbox access, and standardised documentation templates.

**The critical client advisory point**: GPAI enforcement and Article 50 transparency obligations did not move — they arrive on 2 August 2026 regardless of the Omnibus. Treating the whole thing as a reprieve is how an enterprise walks into August 2026 out of compliance on the rules it assumed were postponed.

**UK — Sector-specific AI regulation, no AI Act** — As of May 2026, no AI Bill sits before Parliament. Existing regulators do the work: the UK GDPR (as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025) enforced by the ICO, Ofcom under the Online Safety Act 2023, and the FCA under the FSMA framework and Consumer Duty. Key development: Section 80 of the Data (Use and Access) Act replaced Article 22 UK GDPR with new Articles 22A–22D (in force from 5 February 2026), expanding the circumstances in which solely automated decisions about individuals are lawful while tightening the safeguards around them.
The UK AI Security Institute (formerly AI Safety Institute) ran at least five research papers since March 2026 covering propensity inference, training-data influence, MCP-tool agent telemetry, sandbox escape, and multi-step cyber attacks. Source: [Scaffold Digital UK AI regulation guide](https://www.scaffold.digital/news/uk-ai-regulation-in-2026-whats-in-force-whats-coming-and-what-your-business-should-do), [Inside Privacy EU AI Act update](https://www.insideprivacy.com/artificial-intelligence/eu-ai-act-update-timeline-relief-targeted-simplification-and-new-prohibitions/).

## 🔬 Technical Deep Dive

### Anthropic's J-Space: A Global Workspace Inside Claude

Anthropic's July 6, 2026 research identifies a privileged internal "J-space" in Claude — described as a global workspace for conscious access. The finding is more consequential than most model releases.

The technical mechanism: the J-lens (Jacobian lens) calculates, for every word in the model's vocabulary, the average linear effect that a given internal activity would have on the probability of producing that word further along in generation — averaged over roughly a thousand prompts drawn from a distribution similar to the pre-training one. This is different from prior interpretability tools. The Jacobian lens asks not "what does this hidden state mean in output-token terms?" but measures *sensitivity* — the partial derivatives of outputs with respect to inputs.

What makes J-space structurally significant: J-space components are "read from and written to by far more parts of the network than any random pattern, with differences that in some areas reach a factor of a hundred. This is exactly the behaviour you'd expect from a 'broadcasting' space — a point that transmits information to the whole system instead of keeping it to itself."

The safety implications are the real story. When researchers suppressed the J-space entirely, shallow tasks like multiple-choice questions and sentiment analysis survived essentially intact. But tasks requiring inference, composition, or flexible reasoning — multi-hop reasoning, analogy completion, translation — collapsed to well below the performance of Anthropic's much smaller Haiku model. This tells you where multi-step reasoning actually lives. More pointedly: training Claude to articulate ethical principles when interrupted reportedly improved behaviour in *uninterrupted* contexts, with no direct training on the behaviour itself. This implies that shaping what a model is disposed to say may also shape what it silently reasons about.

Anthropic released the code under Apache-2.0 on GitHub (repository: anthropics/jacobian-lens, published July 2) and partnered with Neuronpedia on an interactive demo that applies the method to open-weights models, so anyone can verify the results. Independent replication: Neel Nanda (Google DeepMind interpretability) independently replicated findings on an open-weight model.

Primary sources: [Anthropic research post](https://www.anthropic.com/research/global-workspace) | [Transformer Circuits Thread](https://transformer-circuits.pub/2026/workspace/) | [VentureBeat write-up](https://venturebeat.com/technology/anthropics-new-j-lens-reveals-a-silent-workspace-inside-claude-that-mirrors-a-leading-theory-of-consciousness) | [The AI Dude deep review](https://theaidude.net/blog/jacobian-lens-deep-dive-anthropics-j-space-paper-review)
