📐 ML & Technical Fundamentals

**Topic: Model Distillation vs. RLVR — When Does Each Deliver?**

Two distinct post-training approaches now power most reasoning models, and understanding the difference matters for anyone evaluating model fitness for enterprise tasks.

**Distillation** transfers reasoning traces from a strong "teacher" model to a smaller "student" model via supervised learning on the teacher's chain-of-thought outputs. Distillation with responses from stronger reasoning models provides strong performance, demonstrating that reasoning ability can be effectively transferred through supervised learning. RLVR improves accuracy — the probability that a correct answer is generated — but does not improve capability — whether the correct answer exists within the model's output distribution. Distillation improves *both* accuracy and capability. The practical caveat: small models do not consistently benefit from the complex reasoning sequences provided by strong teachers — a phenomenon termed the "Small Model Learnability Gap." When small models are exposed to long and intricate reasoning traces, they struggle to internalise the multi-step logic due to their constrained ability.

**RLVR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards)** takes a different route: RLVR trains a model to earn a reward only when its output passes programmatic checks. Instead of guessing what humans prefer, RLVR uses deterministic, rule-based signals to tell models when they're correct. This single shift unlocks genuine reasoning — not sophisticated pattern-matching. The headline limitation: RLVR works best where "correct" is unambiguous — math, code, formal logic. The frontier research question of 2026 is how to extend it to open-ended tasks. The most exciting recent work is RLVRR (Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Reference-based Rewards), accepted at ICLR 2026. The core idea: instead of checking a single final answer, extract an ordered sequence of verifiable signals from high-quality reference outputs — the single dot becomes a reward chain.

**Why it matters for enterprise AI:** The build vs. buy decision increasingly runs through these two techniques. A consulting firm building a compliance-checking agent on a distilled smaller model may be surprised by performance degradation on edge cases the teacher handled well. RLVR-trained models are more robust within their verifiable domain but narrow. Knowing which training paradigm underpins a vendor's model directly affects deployment risk.

Annex III HRAIS (use-based high-risk systems) obligations are postponed from 2 August 2026 to 2 December 2027. 🔬 Technical Deep Dive

Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR): The Training Paradigm Behind Every Serious Reasoning Model

RLVR is the technique powering OpenAI's o-series, DeepSeek-R1, and increasingly every model competing at the frontier on complex tasks. Understanding it is no longer optional for AI practitioners — it directly determines which model you should choose for which deployment, and where the performance ceiling is.

The core mechanism is a deliberate departure from RLHF. A central technique behind recent reasoning models is Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Rewards (RLVR). RLVR fine-tunes pre-trained LLMs using rewards based on whether the model's output matches a ground-truth solution. Without explicit supervision, the model learns complex reasoning behaviours during training, making RLVR an effective fine-tuning method for reasoning tasks. The crucial behavioural difference: instead of guessing what humans prefer, RLVR uses deterministic, rule-based signals to tell models when they're correct. This single shift unlocks genuine reasoning — not sophisticated pattern-matching.

The practical limitation is well-understood: RLVR works best where "correct" is formally checkable — mathematics, unit tests, formal logic. The question that the research community is actively working on is whether this can extend to the open-ended tasks that most enterprise deployments actually require: contract summarisation, risk assessment, policy drafting. The most promising direction is RLVRR. RLVR sidesteps complicated Chain-of-Thought supervision and only checks the correctness of the final reasoning result. The presence of unambiguous ground truth makes such a verifiable dot a reliable signal, guiding exploration toward correct CoTs while preventing drift into spurious reasoning paths. RLVRR extends this by extracting an ordered sequence of verifiable signals from high-quality reference outputs — the single dot becomes a reward chain.

The enterprise implication is concrete. Looking ahead to 2026, AI inaccuracy is the most prominent risk that organisations aim to mitigate. Leaders are seeking outputs that are accurate, repeatable, and reviewable against established business rules. RLVR addresses such concerns, improving performance, increasing robustness, and reducing hallucinations. For AI PMs building production systems: the best question to ask a vendor is not "what's your benchmark score?" but "is this model RLVR-trained or SFT-trained for this task domain, and do you have verification infrastructure at inference time?" The former is meaningless without the latter.
